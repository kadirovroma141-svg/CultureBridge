📖 The science behind the Decoder

High vs Low Context (Edward T. Hall)

In high-context cultures (Japan, Korea, Kazakhstan), much of the meaning is conveyed through tone, situation, and relationships — not the words themselves. In low-context cultures (Germany, USA, Canada), communication is explicit and literal. The Decoder analyses which context your listener expects.

Face-Threatening Acts (Brown & Levinson)

Every utterance can threaten the listener's positive face (desire to be liked) or negative face (desire for autonomy). The conflict-risk meter evaluates how many face-threatening features your phrase contains and how seriously the target culture perceives them.

💡 Common pitfalls for Kazakh students abroad

❌ "Your idea won't work" → ✅ "I like where you're going — could we explore another angle together?"

Direct criticism of an idea is face-threatening in most cultures.

❌ "I don't want to eat this" → ✅ "Everything looks wonderful! May I try a small portion?"

Refusing food directly is offensive in many hospitality cultures (Italy, Hungary).

❌ "You're always late" → ✅ "Could we agree on a fixed time next time so everyone can plan?"

Personalising time complaints attacks the listener's face.
